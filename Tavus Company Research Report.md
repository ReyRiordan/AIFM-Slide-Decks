# Tavus: Company Research Report

Nov 4, 2026 · @Rey Riordan

Tavus is a San Francisco AI research lab that builds real-time, face-to-face "AI humans": video agents that see, hear and talk back, sold mainly through developer APIs. Co-founded by Hassaan Raza (CEO) and Quinn Favret (COO) and backed by Y Combinator, it has raised about $64M, most recently a $40M Series B led by CRV in November 2025.

It started out selling personalized sales videos, moved into real-time conversational video in 2024, and now pitches a category it calls "human computing." The next leg is PALs, agentic AI humans that work across video, voice and text, a no-code PAL Maker launched in July 2026, and Griffin, an October 2026 research model that nearly half of test users mistook for a real person.

## History

Tavus has had two lives: a personalized-video SaaS tool for sales and marketing (2021–2023), then a model lab building real-time conversational video and "AI humans" (2024 onward).

**Origin.** While running product studio Simublade, the founders recorded 200 personalized prospecting videos in one night and say response rates rose 450%. Automating that with AI became Tavus. The name is Turkish for peacock. Sources disagree on the founding year: press reports say 2020, while Tavus's own seed post says 2021, the year it joined Y Combinator's Summer batch.

**Version one.** The first product let a user record one video and generate thousands of variants, each swapping in the viewer's name and details via voice cloning and lip sync, then sending them through a CRM. Tavus pitched it for sales outreach, marketing, recruiting and customer success.

**The pivot.** The March 2024 Series A came with a developer platform that exposed Tavus's Phoenix model through APIs. Five months later it launched the Conversational Video Interface, live two-way video agents, and the company has since repositioned around real-time interaction. A 2026 profile says Tavus deliberately walked away from early customers of the old product; Raza frames that as a return to the founding vision rather than a pivot.

## Products and technology

Everything Tavus sells runs on one engine, the Conversational Video Interface (CVI), reached four ways: a developer API, a no-code builder, managed enterprise agents, and a consumer app. Tavus says 150,000 developers and businesses use its models, and its pricing page claims more than 2 billion interactions powered across 42+ languages.

**Custom faces.** Customers can create a replica (Tavus now calls them Faces) from a single image or about a minute of video, with a consent step to stop people cloning someone else. Cartoon and anime characters are supported as of Phoenix-4.5.

### The model family

Tavus builds its own models rather than reselling others', and names its model families mostly after birds. The current CVI stack is Phoenix-4.5, Raven-1 and Sparrow-2; the LLM in the middle is swappable.

| Model | Job | Latest version | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Phoenix](https://www.tavus.io/blog/phoenix-4-5) | Renders the face and body | Phoenix-4.5 (Sep 2026) | 134 ms from audio to video. Now generates the whole upper-body frame, not just a face patch. Previews a new face zero-shot in about a minute, fine-tunes in about two hours. |
| Raven | Perception: reads tone, expression, gesture, surroundings and shared screens | Raven-1 (Feb 2026) | Interprets audio and video signals together. |
| Sparrow | Turn-taking: decides when to listen, wait or speak | Sparrow-2 (Aug 2026) | Sparrow-1 claimed a 55 ms median response time on Tavus's own benchmark. |
| Hummingbird | Zero-shot lip sync for existing footage | Hummingbird-0 (Apr 2025) | Research preview spun out of Phoenix-3 work. |
| [Griffin](https://www.tavus.io/griffin) | One model that perceives, decides and generates speech and video at once | Griffin-Lite (Oct 2026) | Preview for select testers only. 48% of 54 study participants took it for a real person, versus 2.4% for Phoenix-4.5. |

Phoenix's lineage shows the technical arc: version 1 used NeRFs, versions 2–4 used 3D Gaussian splatting (which first made real-time possible), and 4.5 switched to a learned generative renderer. Griffin drops the multi-model pipeline altogether. Tavus credits Baseten, Daily and Cerebrium for inference infrastructure and Queen Mary University of London as a research collaborator.

Tavus's case for itself is that it owns every model in the stack (rendering, perception and timing) rather than wrapping third-party parts. On NVIDIA's VideoFDB benchmark, Griffin-Lite outscored a Gemini-plus-Anam setup, OpenAI's gpt-realtime and Gemini Live on both tracks. Competitors push back on latency, language coverage and Tavus's enterprise-only HIPAA.

## Where they're going

Tavus is betting the next computer interface is a face you talk to, and it wants to own both the models underneath and the agents on top. Raza's [vision essay](https://www.tavus.io/vision) borrows the term "human computing" from a 2006 paper co-written by Maja Pantic, and holds up Apple's 1987 Knowledge Navigator concept as the goal.

**Stated direction**

1. **One model instead of a pipeline.** Tavus calls its next class of model a Human Interaction Model: one full-duplex system that sees, hears and speaks at once. Griffin-Lite is the first; a wider release of a stronger Griffin is promised once safety and disclosure work is done.
2. **From API vendor to agent platform.** PALs are meant to be named, persistent coworkers and assistants with memory and tools, such as calendar, email and early MCP access. Raza says people will "hire them for their skills."
3. **A wider funnel.** PAL Maker targets non-developers, and consumer PAL plans start free.
4. **Managed vertical solutions.** Ready-made agents for sales, healthcare, recruiting and training, deployed by Tavus with forward-deployed engineers, push it toward enterprise services.
5. **More of the body, more kinds of faces.** Phoenix-4.5 added upper-body motion and cartoon or mascot characters, widening brand and entertainment uses.

**Open questions and risks**

- **Deception and regulation.** A model that half of people mistake for human invites misuse and scrutiny under AI-disclosure and deepfake rules. Tavus itself is holding Griffin back over this.
- **Competition.** Anam undercuts on price and claims better latency, HeyGen brings distribution, and frontier labs with realtime voice models could add faces of their own.
- **Unit economics.** Every conversation minute means real-time GPU rendering. Tavus charges $0.26–$0.35 per extra minute versus Anam's $0.16, and its margins are not public.
- **Two businesses at once.** Consumer companions (retention, trust and safety) and enterprise agents (compliance, SLAs) pull in different directions for a team of roughly 100.
- **Capital.** The last disclosed raise was November 2025. Training video-to-video models on large conversational datasets is expensive, so a further round would not be surprising, though none has been reported.
- **What isn't disclosed.** Revenue, valuation, headcount, and how many of the 150,000 claimed users pay.
