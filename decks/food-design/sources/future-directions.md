# Future Markets — AI in Food Formulation

Can we change what goes into a food while preserving the taste and texture people love? That question opens two markets: personalized foods that fit individual dietary needs, and reformulation that helps manufacturers manage supply risks and reduce pressure on ecosystems. In both, AI helps identify which recipes are worth making and testing.

## 1. Personalized Food Formulation

A person starts with a favorite food and identifies the qualities they want to keep. For chocolate, that might mean a deep cocoa aroma, a clean snap, and a smooth melt. The system combines those preferences with dietary choices, allergen restrictions, and any nutrition targets set by the person's care team. Meal history and tasting feedback help improve subsequent versions. With the user's permission, microbiome data could be an optional input for clinically validated uses. [International consensus on microbiome testing](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39647502/)

**Example — A cocoa snack bar sweetened partly with dates.** AI could propose recipes that replace some table sugar with date purée. Because the purée changes moisture and body as well as sweetness, the system would adjust ingredient ratios to preserve texture and flavor while accounting for shelf life. Dates contain natural sugars, so a diabetes-related application would evaluate total carbohydrates and sugars per serving against the person's care plan. [USDA FoodData Central — Medjool dates](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/168191/nutrients), [American Diabetes Association — Individualized nutrition](https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-S005)

**What the AI does.** It ranks candidate recipes using ingredient properties and prior tasting results. Before production, the team checks nutritional values and ingredients. For an allergy use case, it also verifies supplier information and checks the production line for cross-contact risks. Blind tastings measure how closely candidates match the original in taste and texture, and the results inform the next round.

**First market.** Sell recipe design and validation software to medically tailored meal providers and care facilities. Start with one popular product and measure whether it meets nutrition targets, whether people choose it again, and what each portion costs. Add 3D printing where precise portioning or texture control provides measurable value.

## 2. Reformulation for Supply Resilience and Ecosystem Protection

Manufacturers could develop backup formulas before a key ingredient becomes scarce or expensive. AI would rank alternatives based on taste, texture, production performance, cost, sourcing, land use, and deforestation risk. The manufacturer could then test the most promising candidates while it still has time to qualify them.

**Example 1 — A chocolate coating that uses less cocoa.** Cocoa cultivation has been linked to forest loss in Côte d’Ivoire and Ghana. A biscuit manufacturer could test replacing part of the cocoa in a coating with a roasted sunflower seed ingredient. AI would suggest blends likely to preserve the roasted aroma, melt, and coating performance. Lab tests and blind tastings would establish how close the result comes to the original. Planet A Foods already offers a sunflower seed-based cocoa-free ingredient called ChoViva. Any environmental benefit would depend on how and where the replacement ingredients are produced. [Nature Food — Cocoa cultivation and forest loss](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43016-023-00751-8), [Planet A Foods — ChoViva](https://planet-a-foods.com/en/portfolio/chocolate)

**Example 2 — Palm oil in a biscuit filling.** AI could suggest fat blends that match the original filling's spreadability and melt. The comparison should also include traceable palm oil from sources with low deforestation risk. Oil palm produces more oil per hectare than many alternatives, so switching oils can increase total land use. Compare the sensory results, land needed, and deforestation risk per kilogram of finished product before choosing a formula. [IUCN — Palm oil and biodiversity](https://iucn.org/resources/issues-brief/palm-oil-and-biodiversity), [Study on the land-use and emissions effects of replacing palm oil](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38145678/)

**First market.** Offer mid-sized food manufacturers software and services to develop prequalified backup formulas for their most exposed products. The software uses approved supplier lists, prices, and environmental data to rank alternatives. Candidate formulas enter the backup library once they pass tasting, shelf-life, production-line, and labeling checks. A pilot would compare time to approval, whether consumers notice the change, cost, and environmental impact.

In the first market, a person's needs define the recipe brief. In the second, the brief comes from the manufacturer's supply risks and environmental goals. Both depend on developing an alternative people would choose again.
